## Looking down the nave

The final chapter begins high inside San Giovanni Battista. From the choir loft, the eye travels along a patterned floor, past rows of pews, toward the altar. In a side chapel, carved wood and stone stand on a chequered floor. Outside, the pale facade and brick bell tower rise over Gambarare.

These photographs were made during family research visits in 2023 and 2024. Together with the [1856 birth record](/en/archive/giuseppe-birth-1856/), they bring a name on a page into contact with the village it named.

## A daughter preserves the place

Catarina was nine when she appeared among Giuseppe and Fosca’s children in the 1891 immigration register. On 8 February 1902, she married Luigi Sari in Capivari. Her [marriage entry](/en/archive/catharina-luigi-marriage-1902/) identifies her as Catharina Pansonato, native of Gambarare in Venice, and daughter of Giuseppe Pansonato and Maria Fosca.

That short Brazilian entry carried the village name across the ocean. Read beside Giuseppe’s Italian certificate, it ties the child in the immigrant ledger to a precise place near the Venetian lagoon. A daughter’s marriage record became one of the strongest paths back to the family’s origin.

## The Mantova ambiguity

Luiz Pansonatto’s 1969 death record gives his birthplace as Mantova and an age that differs from the five-year-old Luigi listed in 1891. The [source note](/en/archive/luiz-death-1969/) keeps that later statement visible. Earlier records—the Italian certificate for Giuseppe and Catharina’s Capivari marriage entry—connect the family directly to Gambarare.

The tension between the documents became a useful signpost: one later record pointed toward Mantova, while two closer links returned the research to Gambarare.

## Summer light, winter fog

The [field photographs](/en/archive/gambarare-field-documentation-2023-2024/) were made on 4 July 2023 and 27 January 2024. Summer gives the church a clear blue sky and strong light across its white facade. Winter almost dissolves the building into fog. Inside are the choir-loft view, nave, side chapel, ceilings, and confessional.

The wider gallery extends into the Brenta landscape. At nearby Dolo, a bell tower rises above low buildings and the canal. At Gambarare’s cemetery, rows of tombs and columbaria recede into dense mist. The images create a sense of distance and return through weather, architecture, and the changing light of two visits.

## Memory becomes a place again

The return began with preserved papers: a parish certificate, a Brazilian marriage entry, civil records, and the descendant ledger attributed to Arlindo. Each held a different part of the route back. Together they led from variant spellings and scattered dates to a church, a village, and a landscape that could be photographed.

Gambarare closes the exhibition as both origin and present place. The family name has travelled through languages, occupations, towns, and generations; the word written in Giuseppe’s 1856 record can still be found on the map and beneath the feet of those who return.

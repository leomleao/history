## A marriage in a new generation

On 4 June 1921, José Pansonatto Filho married Assumpta Elvira Pozzatto in Capivari. Their [marriage certificate](/en/archive/jose-assumpta-marriage-1921/) calls José a *lavrador*. He had been born in Capivari in 1899, eight years after his parents entered the São Paulo immigrant hostel. Assumpta was born there on 28 September 1902 to the Italians Serafino Pessato and Clotilde Munaro.

Their marriage joins two Brazilian-born lives whose records still carry Italian family names. On the page, those names are already changing: Pansonatto, Pozzatto, Pessato, and Munaro sit together in a Capivari civil act.

## Four grandparents on one page

José’s [birth record](/en/archive/jose-filho-birth-1899/) reaches across three generations. It names his parents as José Pansonato and Fosca Moro, then lists four grandparents. Andrea Moro and Paschoa Toffano appear on Fosca’s side. The paternal names differ in spelling from the Italian certificate’s Domenico Panzonato and Angela Salviato.

These variations show names moving through languages, memory, and handwriting. Giuseppe Giacomo Panzonato becomes José Pansonatto; Luigi becomes Luiz; Fosca Moro appears as Fosca Morro. Arlindo’s generation uses the form Panssonatto. The relationships remain visible even as the spelling changes.

## Fosca and Giuseppe in Capivari

Fosca died in Capivari on 3 August 1927. Her [death certificate](/en/archive/fosca-death-1927/) records chronic nephritis and names José Pansonatto as her husband. It closes a documentary line that began with her 1881 marriage in Mira and continued through the household entry of 1891.

Giuseppe—now recorded as José Pansonatto—died in Capivari on 5 December 1937. The [death record](/en/archive/giuseppe-death-1937/) was declared by José Maestrello and describes a widowed agricultural worker with ten adult surviving children. It preserves the scale of the family that grew after migration, even as its reported age and parent spellings differ from the earlier Italian certificate.

## From fields to industry

José Filho’s records span a wider shift in work and place. He was a *lavrador* at marriage in 1921. His final record describes him as an industrial worker and pensioner and places his death in Jundiaí on 17 December 1988. The [two occupations](/en/archive/jose-filho-death-1988/) frame one life between agricultural Capivari and industrial Jundiaí.

They also show how family history can follow change without reducing a generation to a single role. A son born to immigrant agricultural workers entered a different urban economy while carrying the same relationships through altered names.

## Arlindo gathers the names

Arlindo Panssonatto was born in Capivari on 18 August 1929, the son of José and Assumpta. The family archive attributes a handwritten descendant ledger to him and remembers his role in gathering papers for the citizenship dossier.

The [family ledger](/en/archive/arlindo-family-tree/) turns record keeping into part of the story. Officials first wrote the births, marriages, and deaths; a later generation assembled those separate pages into a lineage. In Arlindo’s hand, names that had changed across countries and decades were gathered into one family memory.

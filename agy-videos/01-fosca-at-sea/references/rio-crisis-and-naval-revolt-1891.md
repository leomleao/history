## 1. The Historical Crisis (November 1891)

When the *Colombo* arrived in Brazilian waters in late November 1891, it did not sail into a peaceful harbor. It arrived in the middle of the **most dangerous political and military crisis in early Brazilian Republican history**:

1. **The Coup of 3 November 1891**:
   - Marshal Deodoro da Fonseca, the first President of the Republic, dissolved the National Congress by Decree No. 641 and declared a **state of siege** in Rio de Janeiro, suspending constitutional liberties.
   - Soldiers patrolled the streets of the capital, political opposition figures were arrested, and military tension gripped the city.
2. **The First Naval Revolt (23 November 1891)**:
   - Admiral Custódio José de Mello mutinied aboard the ironclad battleship ***Riachuelo*** and rallied the naval fleet in Guanabara Bay.
   - The *Riachuelo*'s massive heavy cannons and the naval squadron were trained directly on the city of Rio de Janeiro. Custódio issued an ultimatum: Deodoro must resign immediately, or the navy would open fire and bombard the capital.
   - Faced with an imminent naval bombardment and armed civil insurrection, Deodoro resigned that afternoon. Vice-President Floriano Peixoto assumed power, but martial tension and armed ships remained positioned in the bay for weeks.

## 2. The Immigrant Experience: The Shock of Arrival

- **Expectation vs. Reality**:
  - For weeks in the stormy steerage of the *Colombo*, Fosca, Giuseppe, and their fellow passengers had endured sickness and terror buoyed by the promise of peace, open land, and a fresh start.
  - Instead of tranquil green shores, the *Colombo* entered Guanabara Bay under the shadow of ironclad warships (*encourançados*), bristling naval cannon batteries, military launches, and the terrifying threat of artillery fire.
- **The Agony of the Unknown**:
  - The family had left everything behind in Italy: their ancestral soil in Veneto was gone, their money spent on the passage.
  - Looking at the warships in the harbor with six small children clinging to their coats, the terrifying thought was inescapable: *Have we escaped flood and starvation in Italy only to bring our children into an armed war zone? Did we make a terrible mistake?*
- **The Transfer to São Paulo**:
  - The Panzonato family's official documentation notes *Procedência: Rio de Janeiro*. They cleared the tense port of Rio and were dispatched down the coast and inland, arriving safely at the Hospedaria dos Imigrantes do Brás on **15 December 1891**, before boarding the train to the coffee plantations of Tietê.

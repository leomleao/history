## Technical Specifications & Vessel History

- **Original Name & Construction**: Built in 1873 as the cargo freighter *Brazil* on the River Tyne (*“Helburn-on-Tyne”*, Great Britain) by A. Leslie & Co. (C. Mitchell & Co.).
- **Conversion to Emigrant Carrier**: Purchased in 1888 by Genoese shipowner Giacomo Cresta and converted into a mixed cargo-and-passenger vessel to serve the transatlantic mass emigration routes to South America.
- **Renaming**: Following a maritime collision in December 1889 and subsequent repairs at Genoa, the vessel was rechristened the ***Colombo*** at the beginning of 1890.
- **Dimensions & Machinery**:
  - **Length**: 104 meters (~341 feet).
  - **Beam (Breadth)**: 10 meters (~33 feet) — exceptionally narrow for its length, making the ship susceptible to violent rolling in heavy seas.
  - **Gross Tonnage**: 2,408 GRT.
  - **Speed**: Average 9 knots (~16.6 km/h).
  - **Propulsion**: Coal-fired steam engine with single screw propeller and auxiliary sailing rigging.
- **Accommodations & Capacity**:
  - **Steerage (*Terza Classe*)**: Approximately 700 emigrants housed in communal converted cargo holds below decks with tiered wooden berths.
  - **Cabins**: 80 to 100 passengers in upper deckhouses.
  - **Provisions**: Equipped with early refrigerated installations for storing meat and fresh provisions.
- **Regular Service Route**: Genoa – Lisbon – Rio de Janeiro – Santos (approx. 18–22 days of Atlantic passage).

## Documented Hazards on the Colombo in 1891

In **February 1891** (ten months before the Panzonato family's voyage), on a northbound sailing from Santos to Genoa carrying 1,027 returning passengers, a severe outbreak of **yellow fever** erupted aboard the *Colombo*. 

Fifteen passengers died at sea before reaching port, and the vessel was placed under strict maritime quarantine upon arrival. This documented historical event confirms the lethal biological risks and sanitation challenges inherent to the *Colombo*'s crowded passenger holds during the very year of the Panzonato crossing.

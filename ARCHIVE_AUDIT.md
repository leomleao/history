# Panzonato archive audit

Three Orca research workers audited the historical narrative, genealogy, and media before this expansion. The scope includes the complete Panzonato/Moro catalog, relevant ancestor profiles, source transcripts, monograph, tree exports, citizenship ZIP, and both local and original asset holdings. Cardross was excluded; original research files were read only.

## What the archive contains

The media audit resolves 780 physical representations into 193 distinct items after exact-byte and JPEG/WebP deduplication. It visually inspected 104 images, including every field photograph. The original catalog's 214 unique local links resolve in the research repository; 77 targets are absent from the website checkout, including research files and 13 media targets.

## The story recovered

The strongest narrative follows Giuseppe's documented 1856 Gambarare birth, the 1881 marriage to Fosca, the eight names entered in the Hospedaria register in 1891, the family's presence in Capivari by 1899, the next generation's marriages and working lives, and the return photographs of 2023–2024. The historical sources add the worlds of shipping, immigration policy, agricultural work, railways, and the Veneto.

The genealogical audit proposed ten historic profiles with traceable relationships. The narrative audit produced forty bilingual facts and fifty chapter opportunities. The media audit selected a varied exhibition set with distinct chapter leads and document, illustration, landscape, work, and interior views. No verified ancestor portraits were found; contextual groups must never be presented as the family.

## Corrections that matter

- The 1891 record is a São Paulo Hospedaria entry: **book 031, page 283**, associated with the Colombo, proceeding from Rio de Janeiro, destination **Tietê**. It does not establish an exact departure port, voyage length, or immediate Capivari itinerary.
- Giuseppe's parish extract records **birth on 22 July 1856**. It does not state the separately claimed baptism date.
- Later Brazilian records vary spellings, ages, and parent names. The website preserves relevant discrepancies in source notes instead of silently harmonizing them.
- Assumpta's records identify her parents as Serafino/Seraphim Pessato/Pozzatto and Clotilde Munaro, correcting the generated ancestor prose.
- Catharina's marriage record is dated **8 February 1902** and preserves Gambarare as her birthplace.
- Several image filenames and catalog captions are wrong. A church portal was called a baptismal font; APESP coffee workers were called a railway platform; urban streets were called colono housing. Visual inspection controls the new captions.
- The old narrative invented farewells, meals, exact train transfers, and farm-contract details. The revised story distinguishes documented family events from wider historical context.

## Integration principles

The archive supplies material for an exhibition, not a repeated illustration behind every section. Each chapter has a different lead and a supporting gallery. The homepage's hero, chapter preview, place explorer, and object selections have separate roles. The archive exposes record types, captions, citations, source links, and readable excerpts. Family relationships and timeline moments link directly to evidence.

Originals, derivative images, video, raw catalogs, and detailed audit/contact-sheet artifacts remain local and ignored. Only selected public metadata, narrative, and code belong in Git. Full modern descendant records and the handwritten tree containing living relatives are not published as facsimiles.

Detailed worker reports and inventories are retained locally under `work/implementation/research-audit/`. Verification of the integrated exhibition is recorded in `REVIEW.md`.
